Common shape facts
- Roughly 100 mm (4 in) long, flat tweezer/clip shape, one-piece molded plastic or nylon (snippet: quickcable, Lisle).
- Colours seen: orange (quickcable); other common ones are yellow/red/black (Maplin yellow short puller, snippet).
- Working end: two small prongs/cup that clamp the fuse's plastic shoulders; no moving parts, the arms flex (general knowledge).
- Distinguishing features: (a) very small and flat, (b) bright plastic colour, (c) spring-open U or tweezer arms with a notched/recessed gripping end. Often snapped into the fuse-box lid by the maker (general knowledge).
- Use: pull the fuse straight out of the interior or engine-bay fuse box, then check the metal link.

Common shape facts
- Long thin tube about 80-150 mm (3-6 in) with 3/8 in square drive hole at the top and a 16 mm (5/8 in) hex at the bottom; OD only ~21 mm for thin wall (Laser 16 mm OD 20.8 mm, snippet).
- Chrome plated steel, a black rubber insert or magnet inside the hex to hold the plug porcelain (K Tool, ARES, Milwaukee, snippet).
- Some versions have a swivel/flex joint just above the hex (Proto, K Tool, snippet).
- Extension bar (6 in / 10 in, general knowledge): thin chrome rod, ~9.5 mm round, with a square male end with ball detent on one side and a square female end on the other, optional knurled grip band.
- Distinguishing features: (a) the tube is long and narrow like a straw, (b) black rubber visible in the hex mouth, (c) chrome mirror finish with stamped size on the side.
- Use: remove the coil pack, drop the socket down the plug well, turn with a 3/8 ratchet to unscrew the plug.

Common shape facts
- Rigid long-reach type: two long steel arms crossing at a pivot, ~430 mm long, short jaws at the end with notched or cross-cut tips; spring-loaded; a ratchet bar between the handles locks the jaws open (Lisle 17000, fetched).
- Cable type: normal plier-like handles at one end with a cable (~700 mm) running to a small clamp head; squeeze the handle to open the head (Draper, snippet).
- Swivel-jaw type: short pliers with small round swivel jaw pads that turn to meet the clamp lugs (Astro, snippet).
- Handles: dual-material grips, typically black plus a colour (Lisle, fetched; colour not given); steel parts black oxide or chrome.
- Distinguishing features: (a) very long and thin arms, (b) the ratchet/lock strip across the handles, (c) tiny notched jaw tips for spring-clamp ears.
- Use: squeeze to pinch the spring clamp, slide it down the hose, release at the new spot.

Common shape facts
- Low wide rectangular/oval tub, molded polyethylene: about 450-530 mm long, 400-450 mm wide, 140-240 mm tall; 15-19 L (4-5 gal) (snippets).
- Colours: oil pans commonly black, blue or red; coolant/antifreeze pans green (K Tool, ARES, snippet).
- Features: a raised splash lip around the rim, a pour spout at a corner with a screw cap, a molded carry handle, often a recessed funnel area, plug storage (Pak, snippet).
- Coolant drain-and-fill (shop type): a bigger unit with a funnel and screen on top of the tank (Snap-on Coolant King 25 gal, snippet); the home version is just a green pan with a spout.
- Distinguishing features: (a) very low, wide, flat profile, (b) corner pour spout with cap, (c) rim lip plus handle on one end.
- Use: slide under the oil pan or radiator drain, open the plug, let fluid fill the pan, then pour from the spout into a waste container.
